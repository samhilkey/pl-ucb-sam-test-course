# Image-generation prompts

Method: built-in image_gen. All three are new generations, without reference images. Generated originals remain in the image tool output directory; unmodified copies are stored here.

## Parabola on plain paper

Use case: photorealistic-natural, scientific educational test input.
Create ONE convincing ordinary smartphone photograph of a student's pencil-drawn mathematical graph on a real sheet of unruled off-white paper lying on a pale wooden desk. This is a synthetic photo for testing recognition of actual paper homework. It must look like somebody drew it by hand, then took a casual phone photo, NOT a digital graph pasted onto a paper texture.

Subject: one roughly hand-drawn Cartesian coordinate system and one U-shaped parabola resembling y=x squared. The pencil curve has subtle natural asymmetry, uneven graphite pressure, tiny hand tremors and a couple of faint erased construction marks. Its lowest point is near the axes intersection. The curve passes approximately through (-1,1), (1,1), (-2,4), (2,4). Handwritten small tick labels on x: -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, and on positive y: 1, 2, 3, 4, at roughly appropriate locations. A small hand-written x and y mark the axes. A small '(0,0)' label sits by the vertex. Do not write the equation or an answer in the image.

Physical realism: visible paper fibers, slightly bent page corner, faint soft crease outside the graph, subtle pencil smudges and granular graphite. The unruled paper has NO printed grid. Axes are pencil lines drawn by hand with slight wavering and simple hand-drawn arrowheads. All numerals and labels are authentic imperfect pencil handwriting, with irregular spacing and differing letter sizes. No computer fonts. Photograph at a slight oblique angle with mild realistic perspective, natural window light and a soft cast shadow near one paper edge; graph remains easy to see and occupies most of the photo. Show a narrow border of the actual desk and physical paper edges. No people, hands, stationery on top of the graph, collage, UI, watermark, decorative headings, graphing-software styling, perfect vector lines, or artificial Gaussian blur.

Output a single standalone portrait-oriented photograph of this one physical drawing.

## V-shaped graph on notebook paper

Use case: photorealistic-natural, scientific educational test input.
Generate ONE raw-looking smartphone photo of a real student's pencil-drawn V-shaped graph on a sheet torn from a lined exercise notebook. This must visibly be a real physical piece of paper photographed on a study desk, not a digital plot with a filter.

The notebook paper has faint blue horizontal ruled lines, a faint red margin line near one edge, a slightly ragged torn edge and realistic fibers. The graph itself is drawn entirely in ordinary graphite pencil, including the axes, ticks and numerals. There is no printed Cartesian grid. Slightly wavering pencil axes intersect near the middle of the drawing; the horizontal axis is hand labeled x and vertical axis y. The x tick marks are roughly equally spaced with hand-written -3, -2, -1, 1, 2, 3. The y ticks include -2, -1, 1, 2. A single connected pencil V has its sharp lowest corner at (0,-1), passes roughly through (-1,0) and (1,0), and rises toward approximately (-3,2) and (3,2). The SHARP CORNER must remain obvious; do not draw a smooth U. Put the small handwritten label '(0,-1)' next to the vertex. No equation is written anywhere. The numbers must look written by a human student with a pencil, not typeset.

Make the pencil strokes visibly human: varying pressure, slightly retraced marks, a little graphite grain and restrained hand wobble, a faint erasure near a construction point. Real paper must have slight wrinkles and a page edge casting a soft shadow. Shoot from slightly above at a modest oblique angle, casual phone framing, natural indoor light, a subtle diagonal shadow across one paper corner. Keep the full graph legible. Show a narrow desk border. The graph should occupy about 70 percent of the image.
One standalone portrait photo, no collage, no inset panels, no title, no watermark, no computer UI, no vector graphics, no uniform computer lettering, no hands or objects covering the graph.

## Cubic-like graph on graph paper

Use case: photorealistic-natural, scientific educational test input.
Generate ONE highly believable casual smartphone photograph of a student's actual pencil graph on physical school graph paper on a study desk. This should look like photographed handwritten homework, not a computer plot, rendered infographic, or a screen.

Paper: one slightly rumpled loose sheet of graph paper with faint pale blue printed squares, visible paper grain, a lightly curled lower corner and a soft physical edge shadow. A narrow border of a neutral wooden desk is visible. The graph is entirely drawn in real-looking gray graphite pencil with wavering pressure, occasional slight retracing and faint eraser ghosts. All numbers and letters are imperfect human pencil handwriting, irregular sizes and spacing, NEVER computer fonts. The printed grid is the only perfectly regular element.

Graph: hand-drawn x and y axes with hand-drawn ticks and small x and y labels, centered to show all four quadrants. x ticks -2,-1,0,1,2, y ticks -3,-2,-1,1,2,3, approximately equally spaced along their axes. One continuous cubic-like pencil curve resembling y = x cubed minus 3x: from lower left it rises, crosses the horizontal axis near x=-1.7, reaches a rounded local maximum near (-1,2), descends through the origin, reaches a rounded local minimum near (1,-2), then rises and crosses the horizontal axis near x=1.7 to upper right. Both turning points are visible. Small handwritten point labels '(-1,2)' at the maximum and '(1,-2)' at the minimum. Keep the graph approximately consistent with those marked coordinates, while retaining natural imperfections of hand drawing. Do NOT write the equation in the picture.

Photography: shoot almost overhead but slightly oblique, mild real perspective and a narrow soft shadow near one page edge, natural warm indoor daylight, subtle phone-camera noise. The image remains readable, the graph takes most of the photo, and the texture and pencil marks make the physical medium obvious.
One standalone portrait photo only. No collage, inset, colored curve, legend, diagram title, watermark, computer UI, glossy digital plotting, perfectly smooth vector lines, printed tick labels, people, hands, or objects covering the graph.
